root@imran:~$ cat intro.txt


3 things to know first




THE ATTACK
A hacker broke into Rockstar Games' network, stole secret GTA VI videos and leaked them online in September 2022.


THE ATTACKER
A teenager from the UK, linked to the hacking group Lapsus$.


THE ATTACKED
Rockstar Games (owned by Take-Two), the developers who make GTA, and the fans waiting for the game.


In one line: someone got into a company's accounts and took what was never theirs.








root@imran:~$ ./show_attack_flow


Every attack follows 4 steps




1. Initial Access
Phishing or stolen credentials


2. Exploitation
Abuse of a weakness or misconfiguration


3. Impact
Data theft, disruption, or account compromise


4. Detection & Defense
Monitoring, incident response, and prevention


Next: how each step happened in the GTA VI leak.








root@imran:~$ ./stage_1 --initial_access


1. How did they get in?


Reports point to social engineering and a work chat account. Rockstar has not confirmed the details.




Hi! IT support here. We're fixing your account.


Please approve the login request on your phone.


It's urgent, thanks!


Illustration only, not the real messages. Hackers fake urgency so you act before you think.

root@arshad:~$ ./stage_4 --defense


4. Detection, recovery, defense




Contain + investigate
Rockstar cut off access and checked what was taken.


Recovery
Takedown requests removed leaked copies from platforms.


Prevent
Strong MFA, least access, never approve a login you did not start.


Detect
Alert on unusual downloads and train staff to verify urgent requests.


Simple rule: verify, limit access, watch for strange activity, respond fast.








root@arshad:~$ cat attacker.txt


Who did this?




Arion Kurtaj
A UK teenager. A court found him responsible for the Rockstar attack in 2023.


Lapsus$
A young, loud hacking group known for stealing data from big tech companies.


"teapotuberhacker"
The online name that posted the leak. It also claimed the Uber hack the same week.


Online claims are not proof. Trust what courts and companies confirm.








root@arshad:~$ ./why --motive


Why hack a video game?




Money
Stolen data is leverage to demand payment (extortion).


Fame
A famous leak brings worldwide attention.


Secrets
Unreleased footage and plans are valuable to rivals and fans.


You don't need a bank to have data worth stealing.
